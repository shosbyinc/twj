WHAT THIS DOCUMENT ESTABLISHES
------------------------------
1. The 2026-27 residential water usage rate, $3.41/kL, and the drought rate,
   $3.84/kL, with the 60/70 storage trigger stated.
2. The metered water service charge, $26.65 per quarter, accrued daily.
3. The wastewater service charge, $189.88 per quarter, as a fixed charge that
   already contains a deemed usage component — so Total Water Services for
   Sydney does not depend on a property valuation and can be standardised.
4. That a quarter is 90, 91 or 92 days depending on which one, and that the
   published figures use the longest. This is why the charge is annualised by
   days rather than multiplied by four.
5. A worked reconciliation of the publisher's own typical bill at 50 kL.
6. That stormwater is not part of the publisher's typical bundle.

WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH
--------------------------
WHICH USAGE RATE IS IN FORCE TODAY. The page gives both rates and the trigger,
and links to a storage figure it does not reproduce. Whether the standardized
Sydney household pays $3.41 or $3.84 therefore depends on a measurement that
changes week to week and is published elsewhere. Nothing captured here settles
it, and the difference is 12.6% of the usage charge.

The precise trigger mechanics for retail prices. WaterNSW's bulk water pricing
pages describe a 31-day lag — drought prices apply 31 days after levels fall
below 60% and continue until 31 days after they are above 70%, with the terms
defined in Part 7 Clause 25 of that determination. That is the bulk determination
for WaterNSW, not Sydney Water's retail determination, and this page states the
trigger without a lag. The two must not be conflated; the retail mechanics
require the IPART Sydney Water determination itself.

The GST treatment, which is Commonwealth statute: see antsa-gst-act-38-285.
Note that the backflow test fees on this page are quoted as including GST, which
is consistent — a testing service is not a supply of water.
