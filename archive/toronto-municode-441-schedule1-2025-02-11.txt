WHAT THIS DOCUMENT ESTABLISHES
------------------------------
1. The metered residential consumption rate in force under the version dated
   11 February 2025: C$4.6872 per m³ paid on or before the due date.
2. That the instrument is titled "Water & Wastewater Consumption Rates" and that
   Ref 1 is the entire metered residential rate. The combined nature of the rate
   is the title of the schedule, not an inference.
3. THE ABSENCE OF ANY RECURRING FIXED CHARGE for a metered residential account.
   Schedule 1 gives one per-cubic-metre figure. Every fee in Schedule 2 is a
   one-off event — an installation, a disconnection, a meter test, a turn-on —
   or applies to flat-rate customers who refuse a meter. Nothing recurs on a
   metered household bill. This is an enumeration, which is what turns an
   assumed zero into a confirmed absence.
4. That flat-rate and unmetered customers are priced per room and per fixture,
   and are therefore a different reference customer from the metered household.

WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH
--------------------------
THE 2026 RATE. This version is dated 11 February 2025 and gives the 2025 figure.
Council approved a 3.75% Block 1 increase effective 1 January 2026, and
4.6872 × 1.0375 = 4.862970 — which rounds to 4.8630 and truncates to 4.8629.
The figure circulating in secondary sources is 4.8629, consistent with
truncation but not evidence of it. The 2026 observation therefore waits for the
updated schedule. On 15 m³ the two candidates differ by C$0.0015 a month, which
is immaterial to a reader and fatal to a claim of Grade A.

A WATER-ONLY RATE. Schedule 3 Ref 2 prices a sewer surcharge on private water at
57% of the Block 1 rate, which invites the arithmetic: 57% wastewater, 43% water,
C$2.02 per m³ of supply. That inference is refused. The 57% is the rate the City
charges a customer who draws water privately and discharges to the sewer; it is a
cost-recovery figure for that class of customer, not a statement that the
domestic rate decomposes in that proportion. Rule 3.3 leaves Water Supply empty
rather than estimated, and a plausible ratio found in a neighbouring schedule is
still an estimate. Recorded here because the temptation is real and a later
reader will find the same 57% and wonder why it was not used.

THE TAX TREATMENT. HST is named against technical review fees in the same
instrument and not against the consumption rates. That differential is
suggestive and it is not a source: Canadian treatment of municipal water rests
on the Excise Tax Act, and that provision has not been captured.
