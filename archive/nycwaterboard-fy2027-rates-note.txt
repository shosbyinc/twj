SOURCE CAPTURE ADDENDUM — The Water Journal
===========================================
Source    : nycwaterboard-fy2027-rates (New York City Water Board)
Added     : 2026-08-17
Purpose   : Closing the minimum-charge question that withheld New York.

WHAT WAS ASKED
--------------
Rule 7.6, introduced with Methodology v1.2, asked what every silent zero in the
base actually was. New York's record modelled no fixed component at all, and the
Water Board's Rate Schedule was understood to set minimum charges BY METER SIZE.
The city was withheld on that hypothesis.

WHAT WAS FOUND
--------------
A minimum charge exists and is not by meter size. The Board's adopted rate
notices state it as a per-day figure for meter-billed customers:

  "A freeze of the minimum charge for meter-billed customers at $0.49 per day for
   water service, plus wastewater charge of 159% of water charges."
   — FY2017 adopted rate notice

  "Minimum Charge: maintaining the minimum charge for meter-billed customers at
   $0.49 per day for water services, and wastewater charges of 159 percent of
   water charges"
   — FY2027 rate proposal

So the hypothesis was wrong about the form and right that something existed.
Withholding on it was correct; the reason has changed.

WHY IT IS not_applicable RATHER THAN A COMPONENT
------------------------------------------------
A minimum charge is a floor on the bill, not an item in it. Modelling it as a
fixed charge would add it to every bill; modelling it as absent would understate
every small one.

  floor      0.49 x 365.2425          = USD 178.97 a year
  usage      at 15 m3/month            = USD 340.08 a year
  binds?     no — usage exceeds the floor

It binds below about 7.9 m3 a month. No published figure moves: USD 28.34 supply
and USD 73.40 total water services are unchanged.

AND IT CHANGES THE PRICE CURVE
------------------------------
The curve at 5 m3 a month was reporting USD 1.89 per m3, the same as at 15. With
the floor modelled it reports USD 2.982, because a household using 5 m3 pays the
minimum rather than its usage. The rate never changes and the price does — which
is precisely the argument for publishing a curve rather than a point.

Wastewater at 159% of water charges lifts with the floor, because the percentage
applies to what is billed rather than to what usage alone would have been.
